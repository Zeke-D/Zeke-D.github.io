Q: MIT Reality Hack is a fast-paced event that harness a variety of talents from 
participants to create something entirely new in a very short period of time. 
How do you envision your role in this environment and how will you contribute to your team?
A:
In college, I studied Computer Science and Acting because I saw a
performative void in the world of technology and a computational void in the world of art. 
I love solving logical problems. I equally love exploring the vast berth of experiences humans are capable
of curating. I see myself in RealityHack as a translater and explorer. I want to be perched between rapid programming
and artistic curiosity. At the event, there will be many techno-wunderkinds. There will also be many
virtuosos. Alone, they will create many good things. Together, they could create many great things.
Being able to listen to different perspectives and communicate across different talents will enable 
us to navigate the possibility space much more effectively.
My background lets me appreciate a wide range of what many participants offer.
I feel I have unique lens which can focus the spectrum of individual talent into a clear signal.
